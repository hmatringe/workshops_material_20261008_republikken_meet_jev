TRIPS: dataset generation spec
Written 2026-10-07. Method: label-first. The scenario fixes the label, then a model writes a trip record that fits.
The definitions are the ones in ../questions.txt. The generator and the auditor both receive them.
Same pipeline as inbox_triage: generate.rb exports prompts and scenarios, a subagent (or the API) writes a
batch JSON, ingest.rb writes the records, audit.rb audits blind, a human decides on the disputes.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Output
----------------------------------------------------------------------
data/trips.csv           one row per trip, what the models under test see:
                           id, date, start_time, duration_min, distance_km, start_address, end_address, note
                         The id is opaque (t + 6 hex chars): no label, no cell, no order in the file name or id.
data/labels.csv          one row per trip, the truth and the bookkeeping:
                           id, business, purpose, difficulty, language, note_style, generator_model,
                           justification, audit_label, audit_confidence, audit_model, status
                         The models under test never see labels.csv.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Quotas: 300 trips
----------------------------------------------------------------------
                  easy   medium   hard   total
business            50       50     50     150
personal            50       50     50     150

Each cell is filled by 5 calls of 10 trips. The first test set is 1 call per cell, 60 trips.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Geography
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Ground trips (car, taxi, rental car) in and around cities within three hours of flight of Copenhagen:
Copenhagen and suburbs (most trips), Malmö, Aarhus, Odense, Stockholm, Oslo, Hamburg, Berlin, Paris,
Amsterdam, London. Real streets and postcodes, invented house numbers, invented company names.
Distances and durations must be plausible for the two addresses (a cross-town trip in Copenhagen is
5 to 15 km, Copenhagen to Malmö about 40 km, airport from the centre about 10 km).

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Difficulty (on the business axis, the benchmark question)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
easy    obvious in one glance: the note names a client, a meeting or a private purpose, and the
        addresses and time agree with it.
medium  the note is short, abbreviated or absent; the addresses, the day and the time carry the
        signal, and a human reads them to decide.
hard    misleading signals of the opposite class, but an attentive reader justifies the label in
        one sentence. NOT truly ambiguous.
        hard business: Saturday client dinner; Sunday airport pickup of a visiting client; café with a
                       prospect; a colleague's home for a work session; home straight to a client at
                       8:30 (looks like a commute); a rental car abroad with a hotel as start address.
        hard personal: home to the usual office (commute); lunch alone near the office at 12:30;
                       dropping a partner at their workplace; a trade fair visited on a day off; the gym
                       next to the office; a weekend trip to Malmö with friends; an airport run for a holiday.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario catalogue (labels fixed by the scenario)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Business scenarios. Columns: purpose | situation
client_meeting   | office or home to a client's office, weekday, working hours
prospect_meeting | a café or the prospect's office, first meeting, note may name a person
supplier_visit   | to a supplier, a printer, a warehouse, a workshop
between_sites    | between two sites of the same company (office, shop, storage, second office)
conference       | to a conference venue, a trade fair attended for work, or a training, sometimes in another city
airport_work     | home or office to the airport or the central station for a work trip, or the return
site_visit       | a construction site, a store opening, a property, a customer installation
business_meal    | a restaurant with a client or a partner, lunch or dinner, sometimes evening or weekend
client_pickup    | picking up a visiting client or candidate at the airport, station or hotel
rental_abroad    | a rental car between a hotel and a client or a venue in another city
company_errand   | bank, post office, customs, a hardware store for the company, a delivery to a customer

Personal scenarios. Columns: purpose | situation
commute          | home to the usual workplace or back, weekday, rush hour
school_run       | dropping or picking up children
groceries        | supermarket, mall, hardware store for home
doctor           | doctor, dentist, physiotherapist, hospital visit
sport            | gym, swimming, football, running club
family_friends   | visiting family or friends, a birthday, a dinner at someone's home
leisure_trip     | a weekend away, a summerhouse, a museum, a concert, a beach
airport_holiday  | airport or station for a holiday or a private visit, or the return
restaurant_private | a restaurant or café without any work purpose
partner_dropoff  | dropping a partner or a friend at their workplace or station
lunch_alone      | lunch alone during work hours
shopping         | furniture, clothes, electronics, a gift

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Variety, per trip
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Note style, per call of 10 (shuffled): 3 no note at all, 2 one or two words, 3 a short note, 2 a full sentence.
  Examples of one or two words: "Client", "IKEA", "Mette", "NB Q4", "dentist".
  Short notes may use abbreviations and typos. Full sentences may name people, companies, topics.
Language of the note (when there is one): about 60% English, 25% Danish, 15% French.
Time of day and day of week follow the scenario, with variation: not every business trip at 9:00 on a Tuesday.
Dates between 2026-09-01 and 2026-10-31.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Constraints
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Same generator for business and personal, otherwise the style betrays the label.
- No real person. Invented companies and people. Real streets, invented numbers.
- Each trip carries a one-sentence justification. The auditor and the models under test never see it.
- The audit is blind and uses a model of another family than the generator (Gemma 4 via Ollama), with
  Claude Sonnet and Claude Opus as second and third auditors.
- Audit outcome: agree -> ok. Disagree on easy or medium -> reject. Disagree on hard -> review.
  Agree with confidence under 0.6 -> review. The human decides on review and reject.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Audit notes, 2026-10-07
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- The Opus subagent audit of the first 60 trips reported "confidence" as the probability of business, not
  as confidence in its answer (0.03 for a confident "personal"). Rows with audit_label=false and
  confidence under 0.5 were flipped to 1 - confidence in the audit file. Later prompts ask explicitly
  for "how sure you are about your answer".
- First 60 trips: every real dispute was a record with no note and a bare street address, undecidable
  on its own. The generator prompt now requires the place name in the address when the note is empty.
- Full set, three auditors (2026-10-07 evening). Gemma 4 disagrees on 31 of 330 trips: 26 of them have no
  note, and 27 of them are business trips it calls personal. Language makes no difference. Reading: the
  local model does not infer "business" from a company name in the address; it needs the note. Sonnet and
  Opus do. That is a finding for the workshop, not a flaw of the records: the records stay decidable.
