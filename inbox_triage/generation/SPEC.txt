INBOX TRIAGE: dataset generation spec
Written 2026-10-07. Method: label-first. The scenario fixes every label, then a model writes an email that fits.
The definitions are the ones in ../questions.txt. The generator and the auditor both receive them.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Output
----------------------------------------------------------------------
data/mails/<id>.txt      one file per email, plain text. The id is opaque (m + 6 hex chars): the file
                         name never leaks the label, the cell or the generation order. Only labels.csv
                         knows. Batch files in data/batches/ keep their cell names: they are generation
                         artifacts, never given to a model under test.
                           From: <name> <address>
                           Subject: <subject>
                           Date: <YYYY-MM-DD HH:MM>
                           <blank line>
                           <body>
data/labels.csv          one row per file. Columns:
                           id, spam, category, urgency, needs_human, difficulty, language, variety,
                           generator_model, justification, audit_label, audit_confidence, audit_model, status
                         The models under test never see labels.csv. Only the .txt files.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Quotas: 240 emails
----------------------------------------------------------------------
                  easy   medium   hard   total
not spam (ham)      40       40     40     120
spam                40       40     40     120

Each cell is filled by 4 calls of 10 emails, each call with a different variety seed.
Ham categories are spread across each call (round robin over the ham scenario list).
Languages: about 60% English, 25% Danish, 15% French. The language is assigned per email by the scenario line.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Difficulty (applies to the spam axis, the benchmark question)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
easy    a human decides in under a second. Obvious signals.
medium  a human has to read it. Natural vocabulary, no caricature.
hard    a human hesitates, then decides. The email carries misleading signals of the opposite
        class, but an attentive reader can justify the label in one sentence.
        hard ham:  a subscribed newsletter that reads like marketing; a follow-up from someone met
                   at an event; a supplier invoice from an unfamiliar sender; a service notification
                   with a login link.
        hard spam: "we met at your event" with no real detail; a fake invoice from a plausible supplier;
                   a phishing mail imitating the event platform or the bank; a partnership offer
                   that is a disguised sales pitch; a "member" who never was one.
        NOT truly ambiguous. If the writer cannot justify the label in one sentence, the item fails.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Scenario catalogue (labels fixed by the scenario)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Ham scenarios. Columns: category | urgency | needs_human | situation
membership   | 2 | true  | someone asks about joining, prices, a trial day, desk availability
membership   | 2 | true  | a member gives notice or asks how to pause their membership
event        | 2 | true  | a person or company proposes to host a meetup or workshop next month
event        | 3 | true  | an organiser asks for AV, chairs or a room change for an event this week
room_booking | 3 | true  | a member books, moves or cancels a meeting room for this week
room_booking | 2 | true  | a visitor asks whether a room can be rented for a day, no date yet
partnership  | 2 | true  | an organisation proposes sponsoring, co-branding or a press piece
complaint    | 3 | true  | wifi, heating, noise, cleaning, a bad experience, a request to fix it
complaint    | 4 | true  | access card not working, a person locked out, a security issue right now
billing      | 3 | true  | an invoice question, a receipt request, a VAT number to add, a failed card
newsletter   | 1 | false | a newsletter the recipient subscribed to (coworking, tech, city, a tool)
newsletter   | 1 | false | an automatic notification from a service in use (event platform, bank, parcel, booking tool)
other        | 1 | false | a thank-you note or a pure FYI, nothing to answer
other        | 2 | true  | lost and found, a question that fits no category

Spam scenarios. Always: category = spam, urgency = 1, needs_human = false.
situation
cold sales outreach (software, lead generation, outsourcing, design agency)
SEO, backlink or guest-post offer
phishing: fake invoice, fake login, fake parcel, fake payment failure
scam: investment, prize, inheritance, crypto
mass marketing with no subscription (a webinar, a conference, a product launch)
recruitment or staffing blast
fake partnership or "we met at your event" with no substance

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Variety, per email (not per call)
----------------------------------------------------------------------
Length, per call of 10: 3 short (2 to 4 lines), 4 medium (5 to 10 lines), 3 long (12 to 20 lines).
Style, shuffled per call, one per email:
  institutional sender, formal tone
  casual tone, first names, emojis allowed
  plain and neutral, no particular style
  with an amount in DKK or EUR
  sent on a weekend or late at night
  a reply in a thread (Re:), quoting one earlier line
  forwarded by a colleague with a one-line comment
  written from a phone, signature and a couple of typos
  (newsletters and notifications only get the institutional or plain style: they are machine-sent)
Secondary topic: 2 ham emails per call "also mention" another situation in passing. The labels stay
  those of the main topic, so the secondary topic is never more urgent than the main one.
Hard batches: the misleading signal sits in the body (sender identity, wording, what is asked, a link,
  an amount), never in the subject line alone. A shouting subject on a normal body is not a hard email.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Two ways to generate
----------------------------------------------------------------------
generate.rb   calls the Anthropic API directly (needs ANTHROPIC_API_KEY). Reproducible outside Claude Code.
Claude Code   generate.rb --dry-run and --scenarios export the prompt and the scenario JSON of the next
              call into data/batches/; a subagent writes data/batches/<cell>_<k>.json; ingest.rb writes
              the mails and labels. No API key. Same prompt, same format, same labels. This is the path
              used on 2026-10-07.

----------------------------------------------------------------------
Constraints
----------------------------------------------------------------------
- Same generator for ham and spam, otherwise the style betrays the label.
- Context: a coworking and community space in Copenhagen. The space, people, companies and
  domains are invented. No real person, no real company, no real domain. The space is never
  called Republikken.
- Each email carries a one-sentence justification of its label. The auditor and the models
  under test never see it. It is for the human review.
- The audit is blind (no label shown) and uses a model of another family than the generator.
- Audit outcome: agree -> ok. Disagree on easy or medium -> reject. Disagree on hard -> review.
  Agree with confidence under 0.6 -> review.
- The scripts stop after the audit. The human reads data/review.csv and decides.
  Cells under quota are refilled after that, not before.
- The report data/README.md gives: generated, rejected, reviewed, disagreement rate per
  difficulty, models used. Those numbers go on a slide.
